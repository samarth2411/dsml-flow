Part 1 · The big idea

How data science works

Every data science project, small or big, follows the same six steps. You will repeat this loop in all six mini projects, and then do it fully in the capstone.

STEP 1

Ask a clear question

Start with a business problem you can answer with data.

“Which customers will cancel next month?”
Capstone
STEP 2

Collect and clean

Load the data, fix missing values, remove duplicates and odd values.

Real data is messy. Cleaning often takes the most time.
M1M4
STEP 3

Explore and test

Draw charts, look for patterns, and check if a pattern is real or just luck.

Do customers on monthly plans leave more often?
M1M2
STEP 4

Train a model

Show the computer many past examples so it learns the pattern by itself.

Past customers + “left / stayed” → a model.
M3M4M5M6
STEP 5

Check how good it is

Test the model on data it has never seen. Use scores like F1 and ROC-AUC.

A model is only useful if it works on new data.
M3
STEP 6

Ship and watch

Put the model inside an app on a server. Keep track of versions and results.

Anyone with the link can now get a prediction.
M7

It is a loop. If the model is weak in step 5, you go back to step 2 or 3 and improve the data.

Supervised learning

You give examples with answers (this loan was paid, this one was not). The model learns to give the answer for new cases. Module 3.

Unsupervised learning

You give data with no answers. The model finds groups on its own, like types of shoppers in a store. Module 4.

Deep learning

Layers of small “neurons” pass signals forward. Great for images, text and sound. Modules 5 and 6.

Part 3 · The terminus

Capstone: Customer Churn Prediction Platform

A telecom company loses money when customers leave (this is called churn). You will build a full system that predicts who is likely to leave, so the company can act early. It uses everything from Modules 1 to 7.

20 hours · telecom dataset (Kaggle)
What you will have at the end: one prediction, start to finish
customer details go in“72% likely to leave” comes backDashed lines happen during training, not on each request.

The five models you will compare

You train all five on the same data, compare their scores, and pick one with a clear reason.

Logistic RegressionSimple and easy to explain. A good first baseline.
Random ForestMany decision trees vote. Strong and stable.
XGBoostTrees built one after another, each fixing earlier mistakes.
LightGBMLike XGBoost, but faster on big data.
SVMFinds the widest gap between “stay” and “leave”.
